HubSpot duplicate contacts with different emails: why email matching misses them

HubSpot auto-dedupes by email. Same person, new job email still creates a second contact.

When someone changes jobs, they usually get a new email. In HubSpot, that often means a new contact — even if you already know them.

That's not a gap in your process. HubSpot's automatic deduplication keys off email. Same email → same contact. Different email → new contact. So the duplicates that cost you money (one human, two billable records) are the ones email matching never blocks at create time.

Who this is for: HubSpot teams hitting contact-limit or duplicate-human cost. Not for: replacing HubSpot's Manage Duplicates how-to. This post is about the email ≠ identity blind spot.

Why HubSpot creates duplicate contacts with different emails

HubSpot treats a new email as a new person at create time (forms, imports, and similar paths). That works when the address is truly new. It fails when the same person shows up under a new work domain after a job change, acquisition, or company rebrand.

HubSpot's Manage Duplicates tool (Pro+) can still suggest pairs later using name, email, phone, and more. Useful — and a different job from “don't create a second contact when the email changed.” Auto-create still runs on email. Job-changers still land as extras on your contact count.

The short version: email ≠ identity.

A concrete example

Dale is in your CRM as dale@oldco.com with two years of deal history. He joins NewCo and fills a form as dale@newco.com.

HubSpot correctly treats that as a new email, so it creates a new contact. Sales works the new record. Marketing nurtures both. Your contact limit (and often your bill) counts two. Nothing in auto-dedupe said: this is the same Dale.

Before you merge anything

A merge in HubSpot can't be undone, so don't treat every name collision as safe. When you review matches, look for confidence tiers — Confirmed, Likely, and Name only — and start with the strong ones. (We'll cover grading in a later post.)
