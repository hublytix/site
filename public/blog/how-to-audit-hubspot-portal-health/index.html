How to audit HubSpot portal health

A practical sequence for RevOps and HubSpot admins — not a 40-page theory doc · ~7 min read

Most “HubSpot audits” jump straight into cleaning. A useful audit does the opposite: it builds a prioritised map of what’s broken, what it costs you, and what to fix first — before anyone merges a single contact.

What “portal health” should mean

Portal health is not a vanity score. It’s whether your HubSpot instance can support marketing, sales, and reporting without constant manual exception-handling.

A healthy portal is one where:

  • Contact and company data is trustworthy enough for automation
  • Marketing contact counts are defensible
  • Lifecycle and pipeline fields mean the same thing across teams
  • Workflows and lists don’t silently recreate bad data

The audit sequence (use this order)

Step 1 — Baseline, read-only

Before changing anything, capture a snapshot you can compare against later:

  • Total contacts vs marketing contacts
  • Rough duplicate exposure (known duplicate groups or tools)
  • Engagement bands (e.g. no opens/clicks in 6–12 months among marketing contacts)
  • Critical property fill rates (email, lifecycle stage, owner, country — whatever you actually use)
  • Active workflows and lists that enrol broadly

If you can’t measure it before and after, you can’t prove the fix worked.

Step 2 — Find the high-cost problems first

Not every data issue is equal. Prioritise problems that affect:

  1. Billing — marketing contacts who should be non-marketing
  2. Trust — duplicates and lifecycle chaos that break reports
  3. Automation risk — workflows acting on bad segments

Step 3 — Separate “detect” from “done”

Finding 10,000 unengaged contacts is not the same as recovering cost. Recovery requires:

  • A clear rule for non-marketing / suppression
  • Execution of that rule
  • A follow-up check that the count actually moved

Step 4 — Check the machines that recreate the mess

While you review contacts, also ask:

  • Which forms still create duplicates?
  • Which imports skip deduplication?
  • Which workflows re-enrol suppressed or invalid records?
  • Which lists are still built on outdated filters?

Contact cleanup without process cleanup is temporary.

How long should this take?

A focused first-pass audit for a mid-sized portal is measured in hours, not months — if you stay in prioritisation mode. The months-long projects are usually “clean everything” programmes without a ranked backlog.

Ship a v1 health picture. Fix the top issues. Re-measure. Repeat.
